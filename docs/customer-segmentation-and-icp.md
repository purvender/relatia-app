## 1. Executive Summary & Strategic Wedge

Relatia addresses the operational friction, spend obscurity, and tax-reconciliation overhead of enterprise relationship entertainment in India.

Rather than launching as an unconstrained event marketplace across all of India or all categories of entertainment simultaneously, Relatia adopts a disciplined wedge strategy:
- **First Market Hypothesis:** Delhi–NCR (initial operating focus: Gurugram, expanding across Aerocity/New Delhi, South Delhi, and Noida as a strong candidate regional market).
- **First Product Wedge:** Corporate dining, executive hospitality, client dinners, leadership team dinners, and private dining room (PDR) bookings (10–30 guests).
- **Core Value Mechanism:** Pre-spend budget policy validation, structured booking workflow, verified B2B GST tax invoice data generation (SAC 996331 / 996332), and centralized finance reconciliation.
- **Future Category Expansion (Roadmap):** Corporate offsites, bespoke gifting/merchandise, premium catering, and live corporate entertainment/sports hospitality.

## 2. Segment Validation Standard

To prevent premature scaling or building features based on superficial market feedback, Relatia adheres to an explicit validation standard:

> **A customer or market segment is NOT validated by desk research, macro reports, or a single positive conversation.**

Provisional segment validation requires meeting all four criteria:
1. **Repeated Pain Evidence:** At least 5 independent interviews within the segment confirm acute, recurring friction around venue coordination, policy enforcement, or GST invoice reconciliation.
2. **Identified Economic Buyer & Champion:** Clear clarity on which specific corporate role holds the budget (e.g., VP Sales, Partner) versus which role operates the workflow (e.g., EA, Field Marketing) and signs off on compliance (e.g., Finance Controller).
3. **Defined Pilot Path:** The prospective customer confirms a practical path to run an initial pilot without requiring immediate custom ERP integrations or multi-quarter procurement hurdles.
4. **Evidence of Recurring Event Activity:** Verifiable history of hosting at least 2 qualifying corporate hospitality events per month within the target geographic corridors.

## 4. Enterprise Segment Comparison

The enterprise landscape in Delhi–NCR contains distinct corporate archetypes with widely differing hospitality habits, budget ownership, and procurement frictions:

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Segment                     | Primary Event Types        | Key Decision Drivers    | Major Pain Point      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Tier-1 Consultancies     | CXO advisory, recruitment  | Discretion, invoice GST | Manual expense claims |
| 2. Private Equity & VC      | Deal closing, LP dinners   | Privacy, high per-head  | No centralised system |
| 3. B2B SaaS & Tech Scaleups | Customer dinners, pipeline | Speed, policy guardrail | Rogue card swipes     |
| 4. BFSI & Wealth Management | HNI hospitality, advisory  | Brand prestige, audit   | Severe GST audit risk |
| 5. Global Capability Centers| Town halls, team rewards   | Scale, vendor empaneled | Complex procurement   |
| 6. Corporate Law Firms      | Client retention, partners | Absolute confidentiality| Chaotic EA bookings   |
| 7. General SMEs / Startups  | Ad-hoc team celebrations   | Low price, casual       | Low budget visibility |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Detailed Segment Profiles

#### Segment A: Management Consulting & Professional Services (Tier-1 & Boutique)
- **Profile:** Strategy consultancies, Big 4 advisory arms, specialized valuation and risk consulting firms.
- **Entertainment Motion:** Frequent partner-led client dinners, project kickoff/completion dinners, prospective recruit courting.
- **Budget Authority:** Practice leaders and Senior Partners hold discretionary project budgets (₹8,000–₹15,000+ per head illustrative hypothesis).
- **Core Friction:** Partners and Executive Assistants waste hours managing reservations; expense claims sit in manual expense queues for weeks; invoices may lack the corporate GSTIN or other details required for the customer’s internal tax review, which can lead to rejection or additional reconciliation work.

#### Segment B: Investment Firms, Private Equity & Corporate Finance
- **Profile:** Buyout PE funds, growth VC firms, investment banks, M&A boutiques situated on Golf Course Road / Horizon Centre.
- **Entertainment Motion:** High-stakes deal-closing dinners, founder dinners, limited partner (LP) annual dinners.
- **Budget Authority:** Managing Directors and General Partners have comparatively higher discretionary budgets for qualifying deal-related hospitality, subject to firm policy and approval (₹12,000–₹25,000+ per head illustrative hypothesis).
- **Core Friction:** Extreme demand for private dining rooms with sound isolation; zero patience for billing discrepancies; demand for consolidated direct corporate billing without personal card reimbursement.

#### Segment C: B2B Enterprise SaaS & High-Growth Technology Companies
- **Profile:** Enterprise software, cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure, and fintech companies (50–500 employees).
- **Entertainment Motion:** Account-Based Marketing (ABM) prospect dinners (10–25 enterprise prospects), executive customer advisory boards, quarterly sales leadership dinners.
- **Budget Authority:** VP of Sales, Chief Marketing Officer (CMO), Head of Field Marketing.
- **Core Friction:** Field marketers and EAs lack a curated platform to source compliant venues; budget overspends happen frequently; accounting reconciliation of fragmented Razorpay/POS slips causes month-end friction.

#### Segment D: BFSI, Institutional Banking & Wealth Management
- **Profile:** Foreign corporate banks, private wealth managers, NBFC corporate teams.
- **Entertainment Motion:** Ultra-HNI portfolio dinners, structured client seminars, quarterly CXO roundtables.
- **Budget Authority:** Regional Heads, Zonal Directors.
- **Core Friction:** Highly regulated compliance environments; stringent vendor empanelment requirements; mandatory statutory documentation for hospitality tax audits.

#### Segment E: Large Global Capability Centers (GCCs)
- **Profile:** Captive technology, analytics, and operational hubs of global enterprises in Gurugram/Noida (1,000+ employees).
- **Entertainment Motion:** Visiting global executive hosting, team recognition dinners, business unit milestone celebrations.
- **Budget Authority:** Country Head, Business Unit VP, GCC Site Director.
- **Core Friction:** Heavy procurement and vendor empanelment processes (often 6–9 month cycles). Difficult to enter as a Day 1 startup without extensive enterprise certifications.

#### Segment F: Tier-1 Corporate Law Firms
- **Profile:** National corporate law firms handling corporate M&A, capital markets, arbitration.
- **Entertainment Motion:** Managing Partner client hospitality, general counsel networking dinners.
- **Budget Authority:** Equity Partners.
- **Core Friction:** Highly sensitive conversations requiring vetted private dining; reliance on personal relationships between EAs and restaurant concierges.

#### Segment G: Early-Stage Startups & Small General Businesses
- **Profile:** Seed-to-Series A startups, local agencies, trading entities.
- **Entertainment Motion:** Infrequent, price-conscious team celebrations (₹1,500–₹3,000 per head illustrative hypothesis).
- **Budget Authority:** Founder / CEO directly.
- **Core Friction:** Low frequency, high price sensitivity, minimal formal approval bureaucracy; low willingness to pay SaaS or platform fees.

## 5. Segment Scoring & Prioritization

- **Event Frequency:** Regularity of client/executive hospitality events per month.
- **Event Value (AOV):** Average spend per event (ticket size).
- **Approval Complexity:** Manageability of decision-making (5 = agile/fast, 1 = paralyzingly bureaucratic).
- **Finance Pain:** Intensity of reconciliation and GST tax-credit tracking friction.
- **Venue Demand:** Preference for premium/private dining rooms.
- **Ability to Pay:** Willingness and budget capacity to pay platform/convenience fees.

- **Integration Burden:** Feasibility of starting without immediate ERP (SAP/Oracle) connectors (5 = light/standalone acceptable, 1 = requires deep integration Day 1).

## 6. Recommended Initial Customer Wedge

### The Recommended Beachhead Wedge
**Gurugram-based B2B Enterprise SaaS & High-Growth Technology firms, Management Consultancies, and Mid-Market Private Equity/Advisory firms.**

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                RECOMMENDED INITIAL WEDGE                                 |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  WHO:     Gurugram-based regional offices of B2B Technology (50-500 team)                |
|           and Management/Financial Advisory firms                                        |
|  WHERE:   Cyber City, Golf Course Road, DLF Horizon, Udyog Vihar                         |
|  WHAT:    Executive client dinners, ABM prospect roundtables, partner dinners (10-30 pax) |
|  WHY:     High urgency, rapid budget authorization, acute GST billing pain, no heavy     |
|           SAP vendor lock-in required for pilot validation                               |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Strategic Rationale
1. **Budget Velocity:** In B2B SaaS and advisory firms, sales leaders and practice partners frequently have autonomous departmental entertainment budgets. They generally do not require global procurement committee sign-offs for initial pilot transactions.
2. **High AOV with Acute Policy Pain:** A 15-guest executive dinner at a premium restaurant typically runs ₹75,000–₹1,80,000 (illustrative hypothesis). Under current fragmented practices, an employee puts this on an individual credit card, receipts are delayed or mislaid, and the company risks missing GST details needed for internal tax review.
3. **Concentrated Geography:** Gurugram features an accessible cluster of candidate enterprise accounts along Cyber City and Golf Course Road, providing an efficient geography for founder-led onboarding and venue coordination.
4. **Tolerance for Light Tooling:** Unlike tier-1 global banks that demand immediate deep SAP/Workday integration, mid-market tech and consulting firms are often open to using a secure, standalone web app with downloadable CSV/PDF tax summaries during a pilot.

## 7. Initial Enterprise Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

*(All figures below represent the initial pilot working hypothesis)*

### Firmographic Criteria
- **Company Category:** B2B Enterprise Software / SaaS, Management Consulting, Financial Advisory, Corporate FinTech.
- **Geographic Location:** Office located in Gurugram (Golf Course Road, DLF Cyber City, MG Road, or Sector 44–48).
- **Headcount:** 50 to 500 employees (in India or the Gurugram branch).
- **Annual Hospitality & Event Spend Hypothesis:** ₹25 Lakhs to ₹1.5 Crores annually across client dining and executive entertainment (requires validation).
- **Entity & Tax Status:** Active GSTIN registration in Haryana (06) and/or Delhi (07).

### Event Characteristics
- **Frequency:** 2 to 6 corporate dining/entertainment events per month across commercial teams (working hypothesis).
- **Group Size:** 10 to 30 guests per event (private dining room sweet spot).
- **Spend Range (Illustrative Hypothesis):** ₹4,000 to ₹12,000+ per attendee (₹50,000 to ₹2,50,000 total per booking).
- **Event Typologies:**
  - Executive client prospect dinners (ABM / enterprise deal acceleration).
  - Key client account retention and relationship reviews.
  - Quarterly leadership and department milestone celebrations.
  - Board member and visiting overseas leadership hospitality.

### Current Operating Workflow & Friction
- **Booking Mechanism:** Executive assistants or sales managers conduct ad-hoc calls/WhatsApp chats with restaurant banquet managers or personal contacts.
- **Approval Flow:** Informal email or Slack sign-offs; budget caps are loosely stated and rarely enforced before spend.
- **Payment Method:** Employee corporate credit card or personal credit card; manual expense reimbursement submission 3–6 weeks post-event.
- **Invoice Processing:** Physical paper bills or unstructured PDF receipts emailed to Accounts Payable; invoices may lack corporate GSTIN or itemized details required for tax review.

### Decision-Making Persona Roles
- **Internal Champion:** Executive Assistant (EA) to the Managing Director / VP Sales OR Field Marketing Lead.
- **Economic Buyer:** Head of Sales / VP Business Development (owns sales entertainment budget) OR Chief Operating Officer.
- **Governance Sign-off:** Finance Controller / VP of Finance (reviews GST compliance, audit risk, and payment terms).
- **Pilot Sign-off Authority:** Division Head or Finance Controller.

### Buying Triggers
1. A multi-lakh rupee dining bill was rejected by finance due to missing corporate GSTIN on a retail POS slip.
2. An important client dinner was disrupted due to poor room privacy, acoustic issues, or billing confusion at the table in front of guests.
3. Month-end expense cycle overwhelmed Accounts Payable with fragmented credit card claims.
4. An executive assistant spent 8+ hours calling multiple venues to negotiate private room minimum spends for an upcoming leadership visit.

### Disqualifiers (Do NOT Target in Initial Wedge)
- Companies with no active corporate office in Delhi–NCR.
- Retail B2C startups with ad-hoc <₹1,000/head casual team outings.
- Ultra-regulated public-sector undertakings (PSUs) requiring formal tender bidding.
- Megacorporations requiring mandatory 9-month procurement audits and custom SAP/Ariba on-premise integrations before any transaction.
- Companies with zero discretionary entertainment budget or strict travel freezes.

## 11. Validation Interview Guide

The following structured discovery questions are designed for founder-led interviews with enterprise buyers and venue operators during Day 10–14 validation.

### Enterprise Buyer Questions (Sales, Field Marketing & Executive Assistants)
1. *“Walk me through the last executive client or team dinner your company hosted (10–30 people). From the moment it was decided to book until the final receipt was settled, what exact steps occurred?”*
2. *“How many hours did your team spend researching venues, negotiating minimum spends, and coordinating menus?”*
3. *“How does your team ensure a venue is acoustically private and appropriate for confidential enterprise conversations?”*
4. *“How are budgets approved before the dinner happens? Has an employee ever submitted an expense claim that exceeded policy expectations?”*
5. *“What is the most embarrassing operational or billing incident you have experienced during a client entertainment dinner?”*

### Enterprise Finance & Tax Questions (Finance Controllers & Tax Leads)
1. *“When employees entertain clients at restaurants, what percentage of the bills come back with full corporate B2B GST tax invoices versus ordinary retail credit card charge slips?”*
2. *“How does your finance team currently review corporate dining expenses for GST Input Tax Credit claims? What causes bills to be rejected or written off?”*
3. *“What is your average month-end cycle time for reconciling corporate dining and entertainment expenses?”*
4. *“Would having pre-spend budget enforcement and direct corporate invoicing save measurable accounting team hours each month?”*
5. *“What financial or statutory reporting would your team need from Relatia to approve a 3-event validation pilot or an expanded 60-day pilot?”*

### Hospitality Provider Questions (Banquet & Restaurant Managers)
1. *“What percentage of your Private Dining Room (PDR) revenue comes from corporate accounts versus retail/family diners?”*
2. *“How do you currently handle corporate reservations that demand customized B2B tax invoices with specific corporate GSTIN numbers?”*
3. *“What are your biggest pain points when dealing with corporate bookings (e.g., last-minute cancellations, no-shows, payment voucher delays)?”*
4. *“On which days of the week is your PDR space underutilized, and what minimum spend threshold makes a 15-guest corporate booking attractive on a Tuesday or Wednesday?”*
5. *“Would you offer a specialized set-menu package or preferred booking window to enterprise accounts that book through a verified corporate platform?”*

### Commercial Model & Pilot Questions
1. *“If Relatia gave you a curated directory of vetted PDR venues, automated budget pre-approval, and generated verified B2B GST tax invoices, how would you prefer to pay (a per-event platform fee, a percentage fee, or an annual subscription)?”*
2. *“What would prevent your company from running an initial 3-event validation pilot with us next month in Gurugram?”*
3. *“Who besides yourself would need to review and sign off on a pilot agreement?”*

## 13. Boundaries & Anti-Promises

To protect Relatia’s operational integrity and legal posture, the following boundaries are strictly enforced across all commercial and marketing communications:

- ❌ **No Guaranteed Tax Recovery:** Relatia does **not** guarantee that an enterprise will recover 100% or any specific percentage of GST Input Tax Credit. Relatia provides structured invoice and tax data (correct GSTIN, SAC codes, state tax splits) to support the client's internal tax review, subject to applicable statutory law, supplier compliance, and independent tax counsel.
- ❌ **No Guaranteed Savings Claims:** Relatia does **not** market "guaranteed 20% savings on corporate entertainment." All savings claims must be presented as hypothetical scenarios based on specific operational assumptions.
